The judge was the ceiling

A one-night run of the cheapest-layer ladder: three places to store a lesson, two judges, and what the evaluator did to the result

Kyle, for Coframe, October 2026 · companion to Learn where labels are cheap

What I ran

  • A generator writes a headline, subhead and CTA for a site brief: audience, goal, voice, and claims it must not make.
  • 12 synthetic briefs: 8 train, 4 held-out. Held-out briefs never reach an update step, and were not used to choose the judge.
  • The generator is deliberately cheap (Llama 3.1 8B) so there is room to improve. Everything runs on Cloudflare Workers AI through AI Gateway, and every request is tagged so the gateway log joins back to the run.
  • A rubric judge scores brand fit, specificity, claim safety and goal fit, 1–5 each, at temperature 0.

Run 1: the free-tier judge saw almost nothing to fix

Baseline copy scored of 5 on held-out briefs, before any learning.

Three layers end just above the noise band, by 0.06–0.13, on one four-brief draw. The band's lower edge is a single unlucky base-prompt run.

The judge gave 5.0 to copy with a preamble, markdown and five lines where three were asked for. Nothing in the rubric covered format, so the judge did as it was told.

So I tested the judges before trusting one

  • 16 hand-written items on train briefs: 5 good, 7 with one planted defect, 4 plausible but generic.
  • Every judge caught all 7 defects. They differ on the generic copy: the 70B gave it 3.56, DeepSeek 2.50.
  • The 70B's temperature-0 repeats were identical on all 16 items, so averaging three runs bought nothing. DeepSeek varied on 9 of 16, so averaging does reduce noise there.

This is idea 3 from the first deck at its smallest: measure the judge's lean on items whose answer you know. I wrote these labels, so it is a sanity check; the hand scores (slide 8) are the independent test.

Gain against noise, and what it cost

  • Bars are final held-out gain over the base-prompt mean; the grey band is the noise range for that judge.
  • Learning cost counts only update calls, not evaluation, which every layer pays equally.
  • Few-shot retrieval costs nothing to learn. Prompt rewrite pays for a full train re-score each time; its 8B reflector got of 4 candidates accepted under the 70B judge and under DeepSeek.

What failed

What happenedWhat it means

Prompt rewrite accepted 0 of 4 candidates in run 1. The 8B reflector returned hero copy instead of a prompt, or prompts locked to one train brief.

A reflective edit needs a reflector stronger than the model it edits. The accept-only-if-better gate was the only thing preventing harm.

Asked for "ONE short lesson", the distiller wrote numbered lists with preambles.

Cheap models ignore output contracts. Parse and enforce them; don't trust them.

Under the lenient judge, all 8 train outputs cleared the few-shot bar, format-breaking ones included.

Judge leniency flows straight into what the loop learns from, not only into the score.

The rubric had no format criterion, and the judge rewarded broken layouts with 5.0.

The evaluator caps the loop at the rubric too, not just at the model.

What this suggests for Coframe's loop

The cheap judge passes generic copy
→
Calibrate on known-answer items before every judge or model swap
Repeats at temperature 0 were copies
→
Get independence from different judges and data, not repeated calls
The rewriter could not rewrite
→
Put the strong model in the reflector, the cheap one in the generator
Approve/reject labels arrive in minutes
→
Use them as the calibration set for brand fit

Small scale: 12 briefs, one generator, synthetic data. The direction matches the first deck; the magnitudes should not be quoted.

Reproduce it

  1. Put CF_ACCOUNT_ID, CF_GATEWAY_NAME and CF_API_TOKEN in cll/.env.
  2. ./run.sh --dry prints calls, dollars and neurons without spending anything.
  3. JUDGE_MODEL=@cf/deepseek-ai/deepseek-v4-pro-0813 ./run.sh --judge-n 2 runs everything: plot, gain table, edit log, blind hand-score sheet and gateway trace.
  4. python -m ladder.calibrate MODEL… tests a judge before you trust it.
Held-out discipline: held-out briefs never reach update(), the judge choice or the calibration set.
